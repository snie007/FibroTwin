This document is structured as a living systematic/scoping review with a methods-to-analysis translation focus rather than as a final publication-grade registered review. Two linked evidence streams were assembled.

\textbf{Review A} targeted seasonality in cardiac size, shape, function, and closely related cardiovascular phenotypes. The highest-priority evidence was human imaging studies with explicit seasonal or time-of-year comparisons, especially echocardiography, CMR, cardiac CT, or imaging-adjacent haemodynamic phenotypes. \textbf{Review B} targeted statistical methods for detecting seasonality, with emphasis on methods that remain valid when observations are irregularly timed, sparsely repeated, or collected across multiple calendar years.

For Review A, four structured PubMed query families were used to capture direct cardiac imaging studies, related ventricular or haemodynamic phenotypes, and broader heart-failure seasonality literature. For Review B, four structured query families were used to capture harmonic and cosinor models, circular seasonality tests, irregular-sampling methods, generalized additive models, and related time-series approaches. Exported records were deduplicated within each review stream and screened manually using titles, abstracts, bibliographic data, and retrieved full text when available.

In the current build, 117 unique records were screened for Review A, 29 were retained in the evidence map, and 24 methods references were retained for Review B. Direct imaging-focused papers are still a minority within the evidence base; much of the broader cardiovascular seasonality literature instead addresses blood pressure, arterial stiffness, heart-failure admissions, or autonomic physiology. This imbalance is itself an important finding because it indicates that direct evidence on seasonality of imaging-defined cardiac phenotypes remains sparse.

For each included study, the extraction framework captured citation data, study design, modality, population, timing definition, phenotypes or endpoints, direction of seasonal effect, likely confounders, and practical relevance to a future CMR analysis. For methods papers, the extraction emphasized the data setting, assumptions, strengths, limitations, and applicability to irregularly timed cardiac MRI observations. These extraction tables were then used to build the narrative synthesis and the recommended statistical workflow.

The present manuscript should therefore be read as a well-documented review build and analysis framework. It is already sufficient to support a rigorous CMR seasonality study design, but some cited papers remain represented by metadata and abstract-level extraction rather than complete manual full-text review.
