\begin{abstract}
This manuscript reviews the evidence for seasonality in cardiac imaging phenotypes, summarizes the main statistical methods for detecting seasonality in irregularly timed biomedical observations, and proposes a practical analysis framework for cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) images recorded in different months across a three-year period. The direct imaging literature is sparse and is dominated by repeated echocardiographic studies in athlete cohorts, while CMR-specific evidence is limited and mostly disease-focused. By contrast, the methodological literature is mature and strongly supports phenotype-level regression with annual cyclic terms, supported by semiparametric sensitivity analyses, as the primary inferential strategy for irregularly timed CMR phenotypes.
\end{abstract}
