# Review A summary: seasonality of cardiac size, shape, and function

## Bottom line

Direct human evidence for seasonal variation in **imaging-defined cardiac structure and function** is limited.

### What is strongest
- Repeated-measures **athlete echocardiography** studies show that some cardiac structural and functional indices can vary across the training year.
- The most convincing direct papers are in cyclists, women basketball athletes, elite skiers, and elite rugby league athletes.
- The strongest direct signal is not necessarily gross chamber enlargement alone, but sometimes **functional or mechanical measures** such as RV filling or LV twist/rotation.

### What is much more abundant
- **Heart failure hospitalization** and **mortality** studies consistently show winter excess.
- **Blood pressure**, **blood pressure variability**, **arterial stiffness**, and some **autonomic/HRV** measures show seasonal variation.
- These papers support biological plausibility for seasonality in cardiovascular phenotypes, even if they do not directly prove seasonal remodeling on cardiac MRI.

### What appears missing
- Very little direct evidence in **general-population cardiac MRI**.
- Very little evidence on seasonal variation in cardiac shape descriptors, ventricular strain from MRI, or population-scale CMR phenotypes.

## Interpretation for your project

This is good news scientifically.

Your dataset may be able to answer a question that is still genuinely under-studied:
- whether there is measurable annual variation in cardiac MRI-derived structure, function, or shape in a general imaging cohort.

## Practical implications

1. Treat the athlete imaging literature as the closest direct precedent.
2. Use the physiology and heart-failure literature as mechanistic and contextual support, not as direct imaging proof.
3. Be careful about causal language.
4. Expect any true seasonal effect in MRI phenotypes to be **small**, and therefore sensitive to confounding by year, scanner, site, body size, and operational scheduling.

## Suggested framing in manuscript

"Seasonality in cardiovascular events and hemodynamics is well documented, but direct evidence for seasonality in imaging-defined cardiac structure and function, particularly in cardiac MRI, remains sparse. Existing direct evidence comes mainly from repeated echocardiographic studies in athlete cohorts, while broader support comes from seasonal variation in heart failure admissions, blood pressure variability, vascular stiffness, and autonomic measures."
