# PRISMA 2020 Checklist for Systematic Reviews

Use this checklist to ensure your review complies with PRISMA reporting standards. Check items as you complete them.

## Section & Topic Item

### Title & Abstract

- [ ] **1a. Title**: Include "systematic review" or "meta-analysis"
- [ ] **1b. Abstract**: Provide structured abstract with: background, objectives, eligibility criteria, information sources, risk of bias assessment, main results, conclusions

### Introduction

- [ ] **2a. Rationale**: Explain rationale and describe explicit research question(s)
- [ ] **2b. Objectives**: State objectives clearly (usually aligned with research question)

### Methods

- [ ] **3a. Protocol & Registration**: State whether protocol registered; if yes, provide DOI or registration ID
- [ ] **3b. Protocol Eligibility Criteria**: State criteria for inclusion/exclusion (PICO: Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome)
- [ ] **4. Information Sources**: List databases searched, search platform, dates covered, language restrictions, information on any restrictions (e.g., peer-review only)
- [ ] **5a. Search Strategy**: Present full search strategy for at least one database
- [ ] **5b. Supplementary Search**: Describe other search methods (citation tracking, hand search of journals, contacting authors)
- [ ] **6a. Selection Process**: Define how studies selected (independent double-screening? Number of reviewers?). State agreement reached if multiple screeners.
- [ ] **6b. Data Extraction**: Describe data extraction process (form, pilot-testing, who extracted, double-checking?). List extracted variables (study characteristics, outcome data, bias indicators)
- [ ] **7. Risk of Bias in Individual Studies**: Specify bias assessment tool(s), criteria applied, who assessed bias
- [ ] **8a. Effect Measures**: Define primary & secondary outcomes, how measured/calculated
- [ ] **8b. Synthesis**: Describe how data synthesis planned (qualitative, quantitative, meta-analysis?). If meta-analysis, describe statistical method & heterogeneity assessment.
- [ ] **8c. Reporting Bias**: Describe any assessment of publication bias or small-study effects
- [ ] **8d. Certainty Assessment**: If done, describe method for assessing certainty of evidence (GRADE, other)

### Results

- [ ] **9a. Study Selection**: Give numbers of studies identified, screened, assessed for eligibility, included. Provide reasons for exclusions at full-text stage.
- [ ] **9b. Study Characteristics**: Present characteristics of included studies (e.g., in a table)
- [ ] **9c. Risk of Bias Summary**: Present results of bias assessment (e.g., risk of bias graph or summary table)
- [ ] **10a. Effect Estimates**: Present effect estimates for each outcome (with confidence intervals) if quantitative synthesis
- [ ] **10b. Meta-Analysis**: If meta-analysis performed, present forest plots showing individual study effects and summary effect estimate with CI
- [ ] **10c. Heterogeneity**: If meta-analysis, report measures of heterogeneity (I², τ²) and explore sources
- [ ] **10d. Publication Bias**: Present results of publication bias assessment
- [ ] **10e. Certainty of Evidence**: If assessed, present summary of certainty for each outcome (e.g., GRADE table)

### Discussion

- [ ] **11a. Results Summary**: Summarize main findings; interpret results in context of existing evidence
- [ ] **11b. Bias Discussion**: Discuss bias in included studies and across studies
- [ ] **11c. Certainty Discussion**: Discuss certainty of evidence (if assessed)
- [ ] **11d. Limitations**: Discuss limitations of review (methods, data availability, conflicts of interest)
- [ ] **11e. Implications**: Discuss implications for practice, policy, and future research

### Other Information

- [ ] **12a. Funding**: Provide sources of funding or support for the review
- [ ] **12b. Conflicts**: Declare any conflicts of interest
- [ ] **13. Data Availability**: Provide statement on data availability (where original data can be accessed, if applicable)

## Key Differences: Qualitative vs. Quantitative Reviews

### If Your Review Is Qualitative (No Meta-Analysis)

- [ ] Describe data synthesis method clearly (thematic analysis? content analysis? narrative synthesis?)
- [ ] Present findings organized by theme or research question
- [ ] Show how evidence supports each finding
- [ ] Include quality assessment but no forest plots or heterogeneity measures
- [ ] Use evidence tables to map claims to sources (not statistical summaries)

### If Your Review Includes Meta-Analysis

- [ ] Justify combining studies (similar populations, interventions, outcomes?)
- [ ] Report statistical heterogeneity (I², τ²)
- [ ] Present forest plots for each outcome
- [ ] Explore sources of heterogeneity
- [ ] Assess publication bias (funnel plot, Egger test, etc.)
- [ ] Report meta-analysis results with 95% CI

## When Not to Meta-Analyze

Many systematic reviews don't include meta-analysis. This is acceptable and preferred when:

- Studies are too heterogeneous (different populations, interventions, outcome measures)
- Few studies available (meta-analysis underpowered)
- Outcome data not reported consistently
- Quality of included studies is very poor
- Research questions require qualitative synthesis

**In these cases**: Use narrative synthesis with evidence tables. Still follow PRISMA for reporting structure, but skip sections 10b–10e (meta-analysis specific).

---

## Tips for Compliance

1. **Use PRISMA 2020 checklist and flow diagram** — Journal editors will check
2. **Be explicit about methods** — "We screened [X] reviewers independently" beats "Studies were screened"
3. **Show your work** — Include full search strategies, exclusion reasons, quality assessments
4. **Acknowledge limitations** — Of your methods, not just the included studies
5. **Declare conflicts of interest** — Even if none
6. **Make data available** — Share extraction forms, PRISMA checklist, flow diagram, evidence tables
