# Reviewer pass plan for ventricular arrhythmias R01 aims page

## Goal
Run 6 deliberate passes before locking the Specific Aims page.

## Pass 1: Clinical reviewer
Questions:
- Is the unmet clinical need obvious in the first paragraph?
- Are the target decisions clear: VT ablation, pacing optimization, Brugada evaluation?
- Does the page sound like it could change patient care rather than just improve modeling?
- Are the endpoints meaningful to a clinical electrophysiologist?

## Pass 2: Technical reviewer
Questions:
- Is the methodological novelty clear without sounding hand-wavy?
- Is the distinction from inverse ECGi crisp?
- Do Aim 1, Aim 2, and Aim 3 have a logical dependency chain?
- Are validation plans concrete enough for a skeptical computational reviewer?

## Pass 3: NIH grantsmanship reviewer
Questions:
- Is the overall objective explicit?
- Is the central hypothesis crisp, testable, and reused by the aims?
- Does each aim have a deliverable and a payoff?
- Is the impact paragraph strong and credible?

## Pass 4: Feasibility and risk reviewer
Questions:
- Will a reviewer worry the project is too broad across VT, pacing, and Brugada?
- Do we need sharper wording about staged validation, milestones, or contingency plans?
- Is the prospective component ambitious but believable for an R01?
- Are there hidden reviewer concerns about data completeness, speed, or automation?

## Pass 5: Anti-AI-writing reviewer
Questions:
- Remove generic hype words and stock transitions.
- Replace vague phrases with concrete scientific language.
- Avoid repetitive sentence rhythm and overly symmetrical aim wording.
- Keep domain-specific voice so the page does not read like generic LLM prose.

## Pass 6: Final compression and formatting
Checklist:
- Fit cleanly as a one-page Specific Aims document in letter format.
- Preserve 0.5 inch margins, Arial 11 pt, exact 12 pt line spacing.
- Tighten word count without weakening reviewer logic.
- Re-check the first and last paragraphs for punch.
