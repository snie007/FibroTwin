# Reading log, 2026-04-22 night

## Focus of this reading block
- ECGi strengths, weaknesses, and current applications
- Current tools guiding CRT and VT ablation
- Biomarker / molecular-marker work relevant to CRT and VT ablation success

## Key takeaways from tonight's reading

### 1. ECGi is clinically attractive but still not a reliable stand-alone mechanistic solution
- PMID 34777827 is especially useful because it says ECGi can be clinically useful for broader ventricular arrhythmia localization but likely lacks the accuracy needed for highly discrete radiofrequency ablation targeting.
- PMID 15649241 remains important because it states directly that inverse reconstruction from body-surface potentials is highly ill-posed.
- PMID 37390586 reinforces that agreement between ECGi systems and invasive mapping remains an active validation issue rather than a solved problem.
- Working interpretation for the grant: ECGi is not a straw man. It is a serious and important technology, but its inverse-problem structure limits what it can infer robustly about internal substrate and competing mechanisms.

### 2. CRT guidance is still clinically useful but mechanistically incomplete
- PMID 37283271 is the key contemporary guideline anchor for physiologic pacing, CRT, and conduction-system pacing.
- PMID 35715087 and PMID 37767743 reinforce that response to standard CRT is variable and that conduction-system pacing may improve synchrony in at least some subsets.
- PMID 35000207 suggests QRS area may be a better electrical predictor of CRT response than simpler ECG markers.
- PMID 31094217 suggests non-invasive mapping may be particularly helpful in understanding CRT non-response.
- Working interpretation for the grant: the field does not lack tools; it lacks a framework that can integrate these tools into patient-specific mechanism and response prediction.

### 3. VT ablation guidance remains heavily dependent on invasive substrate interpretation
- PMID 31075787 and PMID 31706471 support invasive electroanatomic mapping as the present standard backbone.
- PMID 28167086 is still useful mechanistically because entrainment mapping directly links mapping to circuit understanding, but it also highlights why these methods are hard to apply in unstable VT.
- PMID 38262674 and PMID 36607130 are useful for arguing that imaging-derived slow conduction and scar-conducting channels can help but do not replace invasive mapping.
- Working interpretation for the grant: the problem is not absence of technology, but fragmentation of partial tools that do not produce an integrated patient-specific model.

### 4. Biomarker literature is asymmetric between CRT and VT
- CRT has a real biomarker literature: PMID 31681965, PMID 30988339, PMID 39101582.
- VT ablation has much thinner truly molecular-marker literature; the strongest predictive evidence is currently structural, imaging-based, or inflammation-associated rather than a mature circulating biomarker framework: PMID 36607130, PMID 38703163, PMID 33004129, PMID 37819047.
- Working interpretation for the grant: this asymmetry is useful. It supports the case that mechanistic computational phenotyping may fill an evidence gap rather than compete with a mature biomarker solution.

## Reviewer-facing language emerging from the reading
- Avoid saying current tools are weak; say they are valuable but incomplete.
- Avoid overselling ECGi failure; instead frame the problem as inverse non-uniqueness and limited substrate inference.
- Strong bridge sentence for later use: current practice relies on multiple clinically useful but mechanistically fragmented tools, creating a gap between phenotype description and patient-specific mechanistic explanation.
